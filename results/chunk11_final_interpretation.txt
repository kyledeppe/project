STAT 636 FINAL RESEARCH INTERPRETATION
======================================================================

1. NETWORK MODEL FINDINGS
The model with the lowest mean final outbreak size was Empirical, with a mean final size of 51.6609.
The highest mean final outbreak size was observed under ER, with a mean of 78.3576.

2. VACCINATION STRATEGIES
Vaccination effectiveness varied across network models, vaccination budgets, and selection timings.
No single strategy should be assumed optimal across all experimental conditions.

3. STATISTICAL FINDINGS
There were 69 statistically significant Friedman tests after Holm correction.
There were 156 statistically significant paired Wilcoxon comparisons after Holm correction.

4. INTERPRETATION
The results suggest that network structure and vaccination strategy can influence simulated epidemic outcomes.
The Configuration model generated the smallest average outbreak in the overall model comparison. However, this does not establish that degree preservation alone explains the difference.

5. LIMITATIONS
- The analysis uses simulated epidemic outcomes.
- The null models are approximations of the empirical network.
- The DCSBM implementation is an approximate generative model.
- Results depend on the specified transmission and vaccination assumptions.
- Statistical significance does not necessarily imply substantial practical importance.
- Findings should not automatically be generalized to other populations.

6. CONCLUSION
This study demonstrates the value of comparing empirical social networks with alternative network null models when evaluating vaccination strategies through epidemic simulation.
The findings emphasize the importance of network structure, vaccination timing, and intervention strategy in interpreting simulated epidemic outcomes.
